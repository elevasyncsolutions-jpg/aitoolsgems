Quick Pick: My Top 3

TL;DR

  • Best Overall: ChatGPT — handles 90% of blog writing tasks, free tier is generous
  • Best for Long Articles: Claude — writes better prose than anything else, 200K context means it holds your whole blog's voice
  • Best for Marketing: Jasper — trained on marketing copy, knows what converts

How I Tested

For each tool, I gave the same prompt: "Write a 1,500-word blog post about why remote work is better for productivity in 2026, with subheadings, data points, and a conclusion."

I scored each on:

  • Quality — Does it sound human? Would I publish this?
  • SEO — Does it include keywords naturally?
  • Speed — How fast did it generate?
  • Customization — Can I set tone, audience, length?
  • Price — Is the free tier usable? Is paid worth it?

1. ChatGPT — Best All-Rounder

★★★★☆
Free (GPT-4o mini) / $20/mo (GPT-4o)

What it is: OpenAI's chatbot. The one everyone knows.

Blog writing quality: GPT-4o produces clean, well-structured blog posts. The free tier (GPT-4o mini) is surprisingly good for drafts. The paid tier generates near-publishable content on the first try.

Pros: Fast, good at following instructions, handles any blog format (listicles, how-tos, opinion pieces), strong SEO instincts when prompted correctly.

Cons: Tends to use generic phrases ("In today's fast-paced world..."), needs editing for voice, can be repetitive across long articles.

Best for: Quick drafts, brainstorming outlines, rewriting existing content.

2. Claude — Best for Long-Form

★★★★★
Free (Claude Sonnet) / $20/mo (Opus)

What it is: Anthropic's AI. The writing quality is genuinely better than ChatGPT for long-form content.

Blog writing quality: Claude writes in actual paragraphs, not bullet-point-then-expand. It has a natural rhythm. The 200K context window means you can paste your entire blog's style guide and it'll match your voice.

Pros: Best prose quality of any AI, 200K context (holds entire books), great at maintaining consistent voice, fewer generic phrases.

Cons: Can be too verbose, sometimes adds unnecessary caveats, free tier has usage limits.

Best for: Long-form blog posts, maintaining brand voice, detailed tutorials.

3. Jasper — Best for Marketing

★★★☆☆
$49/mo (Creator) / $69/mo (Pro)

What it is: AI writing tool specifically trained on marketing copy. Knows what converts.

Blog writing quality: Produces conversion-focused content. If your blog's goal is to sell something, Jasper understands the psychology. Not as good for purely informational posts.

Pros: Marketing-optimized output, brand voice training, integrates with Surfer SEO, templates for every content type.

Cons: Expensive, overkill for casual bloggers, outputs can feel "salesy" even when you don't want that.

Best for: Marketing blogs, landing pages, email sequences, SaaS content.

4. Writesonic — Best Budget Option

★★★☆☆
Free (10K words/mo) / $16/mo

What it is: Affordable AI writer with good quality for the price.

Blog writing quality: Decent drafts. Not as polished as ChatGPT or Claude, but the free tier is generous and the paid plan is cheap. Good for high-volume content.

Pros: Generous free tier, affordable paid plans, good templates, fast generation.

Cons: Quality below ChatGPT/Claude, can sound robotic, limited customization.

Best for: Budget bloggers, high-volume content, quick drafts.

5. Copy.ai — Best for Social Media

★★★☆☆
Free (2,000 words/mo) / $49/mo

What it is: AI tool focused on short-form marketing content.

Blog writing quality: Better for social media posts and ad copy than long blog articles. The blog wizard is decent but not its strength.

Pros: Great for repurposing blog content into social posts, good templates, workflow automation.

Cons: Limited free tier, blog output is mediocre, expensive for what you get.

Best for: Social media content, ad copy, email marketing.

6. Rytr — Best Free Tier

★★☆☆☆
Free (10K chars/mo) / $9/mo

What it is: Budget AI writer with a surprisingly usable free tier.

Blog writing quality: Basic but functional. Fine for short blog posts and outlines. Won't compete with ChatGPT/Claude for quality, but it's $0.

Pros: Free, easy to use, 40+ use cases, Chrome extension.

Cons: Low quality ceiling, character limit on free tier, limited features.

Best for: Beginners, short blog posts, testing AI writing.

7. Perplexity — Best for Research Posts

★★★★☆
Free / $20/mo (Pro)

What it is: AI search engine that cites real sources. Not a traditional blog writer, but incredible for research-heavy posts.

Blog writing quality: Generates well-researched content with real citations. Perfect for "State of..." or "How to..." posts that need data. Not as good for creative/opinion content.

Pros: Real citations, up-to-date information, great for data-heavy posts, free tier is excellent.

Cons: Not a blog writer — you get research, not a publishable article, citations can be wrong.

Best for: Research-based blog posts, data journalism, "best of" roundups.

FAQ

Can Google detect AI-written blog posts?

Google doesn't penalize AI content specifically — they penalize low-quality content. If your AI-written post is helpful, well-researched, and provides value, it will rank. The key is editing: never publish raw AI output.

Should I use AI to write my entire blog post?

Use AI for the first draft, then edit heavily. Add your personal experience, unique insights, and real examples. The best workflow: AI writes 70%, you rewrite 30%.

Which AI writer is best for SEO?

ChatGPT with a good prompt, or Jasper if you have the budget. Perplexity is best if your blog post needs real data and citations.

How do I make AI blog posts sound human?

1) Give the AI your writing samples. 2) Tell it to avoid "delve," "landscape," "tapestry." 3) Add personal anecdotes. 4) Read it aloud — if it sounds robotic, rewrite those parts.
